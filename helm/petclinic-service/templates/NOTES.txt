Watch it come up. The init containers hold the app container until
config-server, and then discovery-server, answer their health endpoints — on a
cold cluster expect to sit in Init for a while before the JVM even starts:

  kubectl get pods -n {{ .Release.Namespace }} -l app.kubernetes.io/name={{ $svc }} -w

If a pod is stuck in Init, it is waiting rather than failing. Look at which
container is not finished:

  kubectl logs -n {{ .Release.Namespace }} deploy/{{ $svc }} -c wait-for-config-server
  kubectl logs -n {{ .Release.Namespace }} deploy/{{ $svc }} -c wait-for-discovery-server

If the app container itself is restarting, the probes are the place to look —
readiness failing keeps a pod out of the Service, liveness failing restarts it,
and the startupProbe gates both until the JVM finishes booting:

  kubectl describe pod -n {{ .Release.Namespace }} -l app.kubernetes.io/name={{ $svc }}
